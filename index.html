Living Museum

A Sacred Hill Transformed

Before this park, the hill was dense, untouched jungle. Today it stands as a living museum of Indian civilisation — a vision realised by the Mohan Shakti Trust, whose mission was to preserve and promote India's ancient Vedic traditions, spiritual teachings, and cultural values.

Local artisans sculpted every murti by hand. The hills were sacred long before the park was built; the Trust simply made that sacredness visible to the world.

Origins

History of the Park

The Mohan Shakti National Heritage Park was established in the early 21st century as a cultural and spiritual complex aimed at preserving India's rich heritage and traditions. It was founded by Brigadier Kapil Mohan, a retired Indian Army officer and Padma Shri awardee, who envisioned creating a space where people could learn about Indian mythology, culture, and values.

The foundation stone was laid on 1 December 2002 by former Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee, marking the beginning of this large-scale heritage project. Spread across a vast area, the park was carefully developed with temples, sculptures, and landscapes inspired by ancient Indian architecture.

The park offers a rare combination of spirituality, culture, and natural beauty — whether you are a traveller, a devotee, or simply someone looking to explore something unique, it provides an enriching and memorable experience.

North India's first dedicated Vedic heritage park, attracting students, scholars, pilgrims, and tourists from across the country.

The Guide's Words

Stones That Speak

A well-told story turns stone into experience. These are the phrases shared at the most significant murtis — where history, astronomy, and mythology converge.

"Look around you: every murti here was carved by local artisans. These hands shaped gods the way their ancestors shaped the valley — slowly, reverently, with the grain of the stone."

At the main sculptors' display

"Notice the number 108. It appears in Sanskrit prayers and in astronomy — it is not just decoration but also design. The Sun is 108 solar diameters from the Earth."

At the entrance courtyard

"Before this park, the hill was covered by dense jungle. The trust did not change the hill — they revealed it. Every step you take, the ground was already sacred."

Guide narration, heritage walk opening

"Can you identify which deity this is? The weapon, the vehicle, the number of arms — each detail is language. Ancient India spoke in symbols, not sentences."

Present & Future

Challenges & Possibilities

Current Challenges

No audio guides or English signage — international visitors struggle to engage meaningfully with each murti's story.

Several plaster sculptures show signs of weathering and need conservation attention.

Limited crowd management during peak seasons causes congestion near the main temple entrance.

Proposed Enhancements

QR codes at each murti linking to short video stories — bringing multilingual narration to every visitor's pocket.

Sunset and night illumination of the sculptures for an atmospheric evening experience.

School heritage trail programmes and structured guided walks for students.

Dedicated conservation plan for ageing sculptures and improved visitor flow management.
